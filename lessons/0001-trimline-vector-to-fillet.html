LESSON 0001 · 8 MINUTES

From trimline vector to physical fillet

The current add-on solves path placement and body attachment. Your screenshots expose a different defect: the physical mesh edge and its tiny bevel are still faceted.

The key diagnostic: a smooth orange curve does not guarantee a smooth brace rim. The curve, mesh cut, fillet, and shading are four separate layers.

1. How the add-on defines direction

Vertical axis

Z is superior/inferior. Bottom, waist, and top landmark heights normalize the template to this patient.

Body centre

The horizontal axis point comes from pelvic landmarks; the scan-box centre is only a fallback.

Front vector

The ASIS midpoint points from the body centre toward anterior. Without ASIS, the fallback is global -Y.

d(θ) = rotate(front, θ)

For each angular station, a ray starts outside the torso and travels radially inward. The hit is moved 1.5 mm along the surface normal. This is why the line can follow the body instead of living on a flat front/back plane.

2. How one perimeter is assembled

18 upper-arc points → 3 points down one opening side → 18 lower-arc points in reverse → 3 points up the other opening side → cyclic close

The path is one cyclic Bezier spline. Most handles are AUTO, which produces smooth tangents. Ten opening/transition controls are changed to VECTOR, which intentionally creates straight segments and sharp joins. Blender documents this exact distinction between automatic and Vector handles.

3. Where the screenshot defect is introduced

LayerCurrent methodConsequence
Curve42 Bezier controls, sampled 12 times per segmentUsually visually smooth before generation.
CutEvery existing scan triangle is clipped against the sampled perimeterThe final silhouette inherits irregular triangle intersections.
ShellInner and outer surfaces are paired and connected at the cutOne closed object, but boundary spacing remains irregular.
FilletMaximum 0.4 mm bevel, only 2 segmentsToo small and coarse to read like a CAD fillet.
ShadingFaces are marked smoothNormals look softer; silhouette geometry does not change.

Blender’s own shading documentation warns that smooth shading does not modify geometry and leaves a faceted outline. Fusion’s constant-radius fillet, by contrast, applies one radius along the full edge and lets you request Tangent (G1) or Curvature (G2) continuity.

4. The correct target pipeline

one cyclic surface curve → uniform arc-length resampling → tangent/curvature smoothing on the body → reproject every iteration to the body → conforming edge-band remesh → one tagged continuous rim loop → constant-radius multi-segment fillet → normals/shading cleanup → manifold + thickness + intersection QA

This is more than pressing “Auto Smooth.” Geometry must be rebuilt before shading. A good first specification is: one perimeter, uniform 1–2 mm edge spacing, no unintended kinks, a user-selected fillet radius in mm, at least G1 continuity, and radius/chord-error checks around the complete loop.

5. Your feedback exercise

When you test, rotate to a silhouette view and classify each problem:

  1. Does the orange line kink before generation? Report a control-curve/tangent defect.
  2. Is the orange line smooth but the generated outline polygonal? Report a cut topology/tessellation defect.
  3. Is the outline smooth but roundness changes around it? Report a fillet radius/continuity defect.
  4. Does only the highlight look patchy while the outline is smooth? Report a normal/shading defect.
Retrieval check: why can Auto Smooth not fix your first screenshot?

Because Auto Smooth changes displayed normals, not the polygon positions forming the visible silhouette. The cut boundary and fillet geometry must be rebuilt.

Retrieval check: what does the front vector control?

It defines θ = 0 around the torso, so the template’s anterior, lateral, and posterior trim heights map to the correct patient direction.
